# Historical CS discovery frame: provenance before prediction

This checkpoint expands the search beyond the earlier 407-paper topic-period sample without claiming to have read every full text. The local arXiv-derived lexical index contains 198,690 distinct matching records from a 937,531-record metadata corpus. For publication years 2010–2022, it contains **52,362** matching papers. A three-year post-year-end window can in principle end by 2025 for all of these; no outcome labels have yet been adjudicated.

The latest record for **12,507/52,362 (23.9%)** was updated after its publication-year end. A historical model using its *current* abstract at that year-end could leak later content. The frame conservatively excludes these records and selects up to five papers by deterministic hash from each eligible publication-year × lexical-topic cell. It has **1,018 topic-year rows, 1,006 unique papers, and 220 populated cells**. Multi-topic duplicates are intentional and counted separately in the rows. Full counts and cells: [`reports/broad_cs_historical_audit.json`](../reports/broad_cs_historical_audit.json). Reproducible selected IDs and version/date pointers: [`data/historical_cs_year_topic_frame.jsonl`](../data/historical_cs_year_topic_frame.jsonl). Regenerate with `python3.12 scripts/audit_historical_cs_frame.py`.

This filter is conservative, not a guarantee of genuine as-of validity. The retrieved record may omit historical version information or citations; arXiv coverage and lexical terms are not a census of CS. The fraction is of *lexically matched arXiv records in this local collection*, not all CS papers. Most of the 1,006 have not been full-text reviewed, and no source-level contradiction or Paper 3 forecasting accuracy is implied. The post-cutoff search below was used only to check possible follow-ups, never as a historical feature.

Three sampled, year-end-safe abstracts were turned into *new testable questions*, stored with hypotheses and falsifying experiments in [`research/historical_question_candidates.jsonl`](../research/historical_question_candidates.jsonl):

1. [2021 REST API fuzzing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15485v1): is coverage-level feedback better for independently reproduced unique faults at equal request/CPU budget? A [2023 white-box API-fuzzing study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08360v1) and a [2024 black-box RL study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14361v1) are related, but their different methods and test systems do **not** answer this matched comparison.
2. [2020 NVD quality audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15074v1): do dated corrections to NVD fields improve *prospective* vulnerability triage at fixed alert budget, versus merely changing retrospective statistics? A [2024 NVD-user study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10695v1) observes problems in use, but does not test this intervention and falls outside the frozen three-year window.
3. [2020 predictive-SE randomization audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08387v1): are model rankings stable across resampling seeds, projects and libraries? A [2024 fault-prediction reproducibility review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05645v1) is related context, not a direct rank-stability test and is outside that window.

The [first source-level adjudication](../reports/historical_source_adjudication.json) changed the triage: the REST paper itself tested the broad feedback ablation, and the predictive-SE paper itself tested seeds, resampling and library implementations. These broad formulations are therefore **not** novel unresolved questions at their cutoffs. Only narrower matched-compute/verified-bug-yield and new-cohort extensions survive, without outcome labels. The NVD source changed retrospective analyses, but did not measure prospective triage benefit, so that narrower question remains eligible. All three still have **unadjudicated future outcomes**; no Paper 3 forecast score is valid yet. The next task is to obtain direct follow-up evidence and frozen labels before evaluating Paper 3 against simple baselines.

One concrete provenance warning from the REST full text: its abstract describes 351 remote “bugs,” whereas its detailed results count HTTP 500 responses, not independently verified distinct defects. The separate 14 Spree + 75 Magento project-bug numbers are authors' grouped, issue-linked confirmation claims; we have not replayed all 89 individually. The comparison must measure verified distinct faults, not response codes or paper-level claims.
