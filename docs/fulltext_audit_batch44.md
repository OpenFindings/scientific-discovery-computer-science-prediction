# Full-text audit batch 44: broad labels hide different evidence and dates

Updated 2026-10-12. This batch audited the 19 previously unread uniform-random PDFs just retrieved from arXiv. The frozen sample covers four topic labels (`databases`, `model_efficiency`, `energy_efficient_computing`, `robotics_embodied_ai`) and the frame's four time bands. Three records also appear in the lexical-priority arm; this batch counts each paper only as a uniform-random audit. The IDs, versions, sampling cells, and extracted-text hashes are in the source and extraction manifests.

## Sampled full texts

1. **Serializable Snapshot Isolation in PostgreSQL** (`1208.4179v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4179)). The implementation section and evaluation compare PostgreSQL SSI with snapshot isolation and two-phase locking on three workloads. The reported result is workload-conditional: SSI is slightly slower than SI and substantially ahead of traditional 2PL on read-intensive workloads. The paper also addresses production engineering, including bounded shared-memory use. This is a real database-system implementation/evaluation, not a theorem about all serializable databases.

2. **Analysis and Study of Incremental DBSCAN Clustering Algorithm** (`1406.4754v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4754)). Full text presents a proposed incremental density-clustering procedure and illustrative cases, but the inspected version has no reproducible dataset, timing table, or measured threshold supporting the abstract's “faster up to a threshold” language. It is about clustering spatial data, not DBMS architecture. Treat as a lexical/topic false positive for database-systems questions and as an unevaluated performance proposal.

3. **Mapping-equivalence and oid-equivalence of single-function object-creating conjunctive queries** (`1503.01707v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01707)). The paper proves complexity results for a sharply restricted logical fragment: single-function, object-creating conjunctive queries. The inspected theorem gives NP-completeness for the stated equivalence problem; it is a formal result, not an empirical database benchmark. The discussion explicitly leaves richer query/function/rule fragments and related containment questions open. The PDF's front-matter date does not match the arXiv version chronology, so use the arXiv version date only for the frozen arXiv frame and verify venue chronology separately.

4. **Learning Deep Visual Object Models From Noisy Web Data: How to Make it Work** (`1702.08513v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08513)). This is computer vision/robotics, not database research: “database” matches refer to image collections. Controlled label-noise experiments report a critical region around 35% noise for the tested GoogLeNet setup; transfer results differ between ImageNet and Washington RGB-D, and category-proposal WUP is reported as 0.52 vs 0.58 for AlexNet. These are dataset/model/task-specific outcomes; they do not establish a universal noise threshold.

5. **Power Management Techniques for Data Centers: A Survey** (`1404.6681v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6681)). A survey of power-management techniques. The authors deliberately avoid pooled quantitative comparison because the cited proposals use different test platforms and evaluation setups. It supplies taxonomy/design principles, not a controlled estimate of which method saves the most energy.

6. **ENTRA: Whole-Systems Energy Transparency** (`1606.04074v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04074)). The paper describes static energy modeling combined with program analysis and system-level attribution. Its discussion makes the hardware/software layer and analysis-vs-modeling trade-offs central; it is not a common cross-platform benchmark with a single independently measured energy outcome. Treat modeled attribution and wall-power measurement as distinct evidence.

7. **Green Data Centers: A Survey, Perspectives, and Future Directions** (`1608.00687v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00687)). Broad survey covering utilization, resource management, thermal control, and metrics. The cited savings and performance figures come from heterogeneous proposals and baselines; the survey does not re-run them under one workload or measurement boundary. Useful background, not independent replication or a pooled effect estimate.

8. **Zydeco-Style Spike Sorting Low Power VLSI Architecture for IoT BCI Implants** (`2209.04427v3`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04427)). Proposes and simulates a Verilog spike-sorting architecture against stated accuracy/power constraints. The inspected evidence is design/simulation-level, not fabricated-silicon, implant, or clinical measurement. Claimed power/accuracy targets must not be presented as observed device performance.

9. **Reducing Memory Requirements for the IPU using Butterfly Factorizations** (`2309.08946v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08946)). The benchmark reduces parameters by about 98.5% in its tested factorization configuration, but added computation and device matter: the single-hidden-layer CIFAR-10 study reports different timing/accuracy trade-offs for GPU and IPU, and PixelFly's relative result changes direction across those devices. The paper discusses memory bottlenecks and configuration sensitivity. This supports hardware/workload conditionality, not a general claim that factorization makes training faster or more accurate.

10. **Constructive community race: full-density spiking neural network model drives neuromorphic computing** (`2505.21185v3`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21185)). This review compares implementations of a shared spiking-neural model and proposes a benchmarking recipe. It distinguishes real-time factor from parallel speedup and defines energy per synaptic event using wall-socket power including required communication infrastructure. Its checklist recommends ten random seeds, specified warm-up/duration/accuracy settings, and explicit external drive. The authors also discuss excluded facility-level costs and model/software adaptation. This is strong methodological evidence for a *specified benchmark*, not proof that every field uses those boundaries.

11. **Harvesting energy consumption on European HPC systems: Sharing Experience from the CEEC project** (`2511.03029v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03029)). Reports real HPC energy/time measurements across applications and systems. In one MareNostrum5 solver experiment, runs were repeated ten times and median energy was reported; other sections use system-specific tools (e.g., EAR/SLURM or platform telemetry) and report scaling/workload-dependent outcomes. The concrete measurements show that matched precision/tolerance choices can reduce both time and energy in the tested case. They do not make results interchangeable across machines, solvers, node counts, or energy sensors.

12. **A Taxonomy and Survey of Energy-Efficient Data Centers and Cloud Computing Systems** (`1007.0066v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.0066)). A 2010 survey/taxonomy rather than a single controlled intervention. It organizes energy-efficiency approaches and metrics; claims summarized from cited studies retain their original platform and workload boundaries. This source also shares the frozen frame's `model_efficiency` label but is clearly data-center systems literature.

13. **Towards Ultrahigh Dimensional Feature Selection for Big Data** (`1209.5260v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.5260)). The paper gives algorithmic convergence/complexity analysis and reports experiments on synthetic and real high-dimensional datasets. It compares feature-selection methods under particular losses, regularization, feature expansions, and dataset conditions; reported runtime gains (including large ratios against selected baselines) are not a universal hardware-energy result. Keep statistical quality, runtime, and the chosen feature expansion separate.

14. **DeltaTree: A Practical Locality-aware Concurrent Search Tree** (`1312.2628v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2628)). Implements a concurrent search-tree design and evaluates it against AVL/red-black and related structures under specified contention/workloads. The headline improvements (up to 5x search and 1.6x update versus selected baselines) are maxima in those tests; maintenance can block, and the paper's own experimental discussion narrows the win to tested locality/contention conditions. It is not ML model efficiency despite the frame label.

15. **Fundamentals of Mathematical Theory of Emotional Robots** (`1011.1841v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1841)). A long mathematical/conceptual monograph defining an emotional-robot framework. The inspected text does not give a controlled physical-robot evaluation or a measured task outcome. Treat as weakly related/lexical retrieval for embodied-AI performance, not empirical robot evidence.

16. **Adaptive Fractional PID Controller for Robot Manipulator** (`1206.2027v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2027)). Studies a two-link manipulator with an adaptive fractional PID controller and genetic-algorithm tuning in simulation. Its convergence statement is conditional on bounded learning-stage error; no physical manipulator validation is reported in the inspected text. The cited venue chronology predates the arXiv upload, a second warning against equating arXiv year with publication year.

17. **Rao-Blackwellised Particle Filtering for Dynamic Bayesian Networks** (`1301.3853v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3853)). A formal/sequential-inference method paper with examples including online regression and robot localization/mapping; it compares Rao-Blackwellised and standard particle filtering under the paper's models. The original UAI publication is 2000 although this arXiv record was submitted in 2013. That 13-year gap is directly material to any historical model that uses arXiv year as the outcome/publication timestamp.

18. **Unsupervised Learning of Dense Optical Flow, Depth and Egomotion from Sparse Event Data** (`1809.08625v2`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08625)). Evaluates an event-camera convolutional model on MVSEC and reports real-time-rate performance on a specified GPU, alongside flow/depth/egomotion metrics. The discussion lists failure cases and sensor/LiDAR limitations; one-device speed does not establish edge-device energy or robust performance under all scenes.

19. **Multi Modal Semantic Segmentation using Synthetic Data** (`1910.13676v1`; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13676)). Uses CARLA-generated multimodal data and tests transfer to real datasets. The paper itself describes the study as preliminary and reports class-specific transfer errors (including fence/car confusion); synthetic training is not equivalent to broad real-world generalization or physical-robot validation.

## Cross-paper evidence and candidate findings

The energy label does not identify one comparable outcome. In the audited sample, the evidence ranges from surveys that decline pooled quantitative comparison (2014/2016), through static/program-analysis energy attribution, RTL simulation, accelerator timing/accuracy tests, standardized model benchmarking, to repeated on-system HPC measurements. A particularly useful contrast is the IPU factorization paper: parameter reduction is large, yet time/accuracy depend on accelerator and configuration. The spiking-network review and CEEC HPC study show how controlled boundaries, repeated runs, workload/precision choices, and sensor/tooling affect what an energy claim means. Conversely, SSI, CEEC's matched precision/tolerance comparison, and feature-selection tests show that within a fixed workload/platform and baseline, meaningful empirical comparisons *are* possible. Do not collapse this into “energy results are unreliable”; the testable issue is transportability beyond the measured setting.

**Candidate finding A — accelerator efficiency may be conditional, not portable.** In the sampled IPU study, memory/parameter reduction coexists with device-dependent timing/accuracy trade-offs; the CEEC measurements and spiking-network benchmarking review independently emphasize workload, measurement boundary, and repeated-run choices. Counterevidence: CEEC reports a consistent mixed-precision time-and-energy advantage in its controlled single-node Neko case, and database SSI reports comparative results across three workloads. A preregistered test should reproduce a set of claimed energy/performance wins on at least two accelerators and two workloads, measure wall power with a declared boundary, use repeated runs, and report quality plus time-to-solution/energy-to-solution. The lead is weakened if method rankings remain stable across matched platforms/workloads and independent replications. This sample is not a prevalence or novelty estimate.

**Candidate finding B — arXiv year is a risky historical label.** In this frozen sample, the Rao–Blackwellised particle-filtering article is arXiv-submitted in 2013 but appeared at UAI in 2000; the adaptive robot-control paper cites a 2010 venue while its arXiv record is from 2012. The earlier corpus audit found another 2011 arXiv record citing a 2000 JAIR publication (`1106.0235`). This establishes concrete label discrepancies, not their frequency. A falsifiable audit would cross-check first-publication dates for a random sample of arXiv records, quantify year deltas by topic/era, then rerun historical outcome prediction using arXiv date versus verified first-publication date with features frozen before each timestamp. If conclusions and rankings are invariant, the practical leakage concern is weakened. Venue chronology and duplicate/version handling need manual adjudication.

## Audit boundary

All 19 PDFs were extracted and the exact uploaded objects were downloaded back from R2 and SHA-256 checked. Full-text review covers the frozen uniform-random arm only; three papers also have lexical-priority memberships, but this batch does not use those as additional independent observations. The sample is limited to four topic labels and the specified frame/time cutoffs, and it cannot estimate how common any evidence/reporting pattern is in all computer science. Venue chronology was noted only for the explicit examples above; it remains unverified for most sources. No systematic citation search or independent reproduction was performed in this batch.
