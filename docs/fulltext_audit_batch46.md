# Full-text audit batch 46: coverage, bug reproduction, and fail detection are different outcomes

Updated 2026-10-12. This batch closes the three then-unreviewed lexical-priority items in the frozen `software_testing × 2025–2026-through-10-11` cell. The papers concern different steps of the testing pipeline, so their percentages are not pooled or directly compared. Full texts were read from arXiv HTML and PDFs; versions, exact sample memberships, access, extraction, and checksums are logged in `data/fulltext_review_ledger_batch46.jsonl` and the download/extraction manifests.

## Sampled full texts

1. **DiffuTester: Accelerating Unit Test Generation for Diffusion LLMs via Mining Structural Pattern** (`2509.24975v4`, arXiv version dated 2026-09-12; [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24975)). Tests two 7B diffusion LLMs on 210-program TestEval subsets in Python, C++, and Java, plus 135 buildable single-function instances from the real-bug Tests4Py corpus. Primary testing outcome is line coverage, alongside time/TFLOPs/throughput; it reports up to 2–3× speedups at similar coverage. Example: DiffuCoder on TestEval-C++ at batch size 3 goes from 14.4 s to 5.95 s (about 2.42×, table rounding) and line coverage is compared at equal time; on Tests4Py, speedup is roughly 1.85–1.95× with line coverage between 26.3% and 30.5% across generation counts (Sections 4–5, Tables 1–2). Appendix quality checks also report syntax correctness and pass@1 for separate code-generation/translation tasks. These are useful efficiency/coverage and artifact-validity results. The paper does not report the rate at which generated tests expose the known real bugs in Tests4Py; execution coverage is not itself a fault-detection result. The authors limit breadth to two dLLMs/tasks due to resources (Section 7). This distinction is about what the experiment measures, not a claim that DiffuTester fails to find faults.

2. **Test Case Generation from Bug Reports via Large Language Models: A Cognitive Layered Evaluation Framework** (`2510.05365v1`, arXiv version dated 2025-10-06; [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05365)). Defines a bug-reproducing test (BRT) as one that compiles, fails on the buggy version, and passes on the fixed version, a more direct fault-reproduction endpoint than coverage. On Defects4J and GitHub Recent Bugs, GPT-4o reproduces 36.5% and 32.3% at baseline; StarCoder is 24.1% and 16.1%. Identifier masking materially lowers BRT rates: e.g., StarCoder/Defects4J 24.1%→6.9% under hash masking, while GPT-4o/Defects4J 36.5%→19.7–22.4% across listed identifier variants (Tables 2 and 4). Open-book examples improve some conditions by up to 3×, but this depends on closely matching bug/test examples. The authors flag possible training exposure, find 47 of 812 Defects4J reports uninformative and 66 bugs grouped under 29 shared reports, and only execute the case study layers Remember/Understand/Apply/Analyze—not the full six-layer proposal (Sections 4–5). Thus even direct BRT results are benchmark- and cue-sensitive, not proof of broad reasoning or repository-level deployment success.

3. **Fail-Aware and Explainable Test Oracle Prediction** (`2607.11342v1`, arXiv version dated 2026-07-13; [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11342)). Evaluates a classifier that predicts Pass/Fail for a test prefix and method-under-test. On the in-domain Defects4J-derived test split, FOCAL has 97.65% accuracy and 97.61% Fail F1. On the 25-project unseen TOGA benchmark, where only 9.1% of 229,401 pairs are Fail, the prior SEER baseline has higher aggregate accuracy (86.72%) but only 2.95% Fail recall; FOCAL trades aggregate accuracy down to 81.99% while raising Fail recall to 23.32% and Fail F1 to 19.04% (Tables I–III). That is a large relative recall gain but still misses about three quarters of Fail cases. Limitations: some Fail labels are mutation-constructed; class balance differs sharply; explanation faithfulness checks do not show usefulness to developers; and the authors describe the result as preliminary (Section VI). This demonstrates why accuracy and fault-detection sensitivity must be reported separately, without implying that the new model solves unseen-project oracle prediction.

## Cross-paper check and bounded finding

The three studies measure three distinct endpoints: (a) how quickly generated tests execute code (coverage), (b) whether a generated test reproduces a known regression (BRT), and (c) whether a learned oracle identifies a failing test (Fail recall/F1). The existing batch-40 Android study supplies a fourth operational counterweight: its integrated GPT-4o oracle reports 49% correct bug-and-description detection with 59% false positives, and the higher-recall configuration reaches 63% detection with 93% false positives; only four of 24 candidates in a separate app sweep were confirmed/fixed ([audit](fulltext_audit_batch40.md#5-a-study-of-using-multimodal-llms-for-non-crash-functional-bug-detection-in-android-apps)). This supports counting triage cost and confirmed faults, not alerts alone.

This does **not** show that coverage is useless or that direct fault metrics are universally superior. DiffuTester answers a decoding-efficiency question and its results are positive for that declared endpoint. The BRT and FOCAL studies show that direct bug outcomes can themselves be sensitive to contamination, identifier cues, label construction, and class imbalance. The bounded research-design lead is therefore: **a test-generation system's coverage/throughput score should not be interpreted as fault-finding ability unless a study measures their relationship on the same generated suites and held-out faults.**

Falsifiable test: freeze the same test generators, target repositories, and compute budget; for each generated suite, measure syntax/build validity, line/branch coverage, mutation score, reproduction of independently curated real bugs (fail buggy/pass fixed), and oracle Fail precision/recall, plus confirmed unique faults per engineer-hour. Stratify projects by age and hold out entire repositories; repeat stochastic runs. Estimate whether coverage/throughput predicts real-bug reproduction and whether rankings remain stable after false-positive review cost. The lead is weakened if coverage reliably predicts held-out, independently confirmed fault yield and ranking across projects, or if adding direct fault metrics does not change system selection. No such common evaluation was run here.

## Boundary

Three purposively selected full-text records from one lexical-priority cell and one earlier random-arm comparator do not establish field prevalence or novelty. Their tasks and datasets differ; no shared implementation, correlation coefficient, pooled effect, or independent replication was conducted. The objective remains the larger 198,690-candidate stratified review.
