# Full-text audit batch 54: private query processing, relational DP synthesis, and vector search

Updated 2026-10-12. This closes the three lexical-priority records in the frozen `databases × 2020–2024` frame cell. The records span distinct database problems and are not pooled into one effect estimate. Three sampled arXiv PDFs were retrieved and section-audited. Primary follow-up sources were checked to correct the papers' open-problem and comparison boundaries.

## Sampled papers

### 1. Adore: Differentially Oblivious Relational Database Operators (arXiv:2212.05176v3; PVLDB 2023)

[arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05176); [PVLDB paper](https://doi.org/10.14778/3574245.3574267); local PDF: `data/fulltexts/arxiv/2212.05176v3.pdf` (20 pages); extracted text: `data/fulltexts/text/2212.05176v3.txt`.

Adore implements differentially-oblivious selection/projection, grouping/aggregation, and foreign-key join, and proves access-pattern guarantees for its stated same-size, one-record-neighbor model. On an Intel SGX/i7-9700 prototype and Big Data Benchmark operator workloads, it reports up to 7.4× over fully-oblivious ObliDB for one foreign-key join case. The same join is reported as 98.1–182.1× slower than non-encrypted Spark SQL; differentials are not comparable privacy guarantees. Composition of realistic multi-operator SQL workloads is explicitly outside its experiment. The threat boundary matters: the paper does not hide all private-enclave cache accesses and says known SGX side channels are out of scope; the speedup is not evidence that SGX is intrinsically secure or that a complete DBMS is solved.

Doquet (PVLDB 2023) is a direct follow-up, not a contradiction: it adds private indexes, range selection, foreign-key and many-to-many joins, and a composed select-join, with a stated differential-oblivious guarantee even when private-memory accesses are eavesdropped on. Its abstract reports up to an order-of-magnitude speedup against fully and differentially oblivious alternatives. But Doquet assumes static data, explicitly leaves updates to future work, and flags selecting indexes and dividing a total privacy budget as open system-management problems. Thus the precise gap is no longer “can DO support composed queries?”; it is how to plan and maintain a realistic workload under an explicit leakage/privacy budget and changing data.

### 2. PrivLava: Synthesizing Relational Data with Foreign Keys under Differential Privacy (arXiv:2304.04545v1; SIGMOD/PODS 2023)

[arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04545); [peer-reviewed publication record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589287); local PDF: `data/fulltexts/arxiv/2304.04545v1.pdf` (18 pages); extracted text: `data/fulltexts/text/2304.04545v1.txt`.

PrivLava uses graphical models with latent variables to represent correlations induced by foreign keys. It handles foreign-key DAGs and mixtures of public/private relations, and evaluates aggregate-query error on California and Île-de-France census data plus a modified TPC-H benchmark. Its reported synthesis runtime is up to 12 hours on a 32-core CPU/Nvidia A10 system; the authors frame this as a one-time release cost. The explicit limitation is data-hungry DP estimation: many observed and latent marginals consume privacy budget, and Gaussian noise can overwhelm signal on small databases, particularly for latent marginals. Results are aggregate-query utility, not proof that every downstream workload or database constraint is preserved.

PrivPetal (SIGMOD/PACMMOD 2025; arXiv:2503.22970) directly updates the comparison. Its authors identify PrivLava's repeated EM iterations as a potentially prohibitive privacy-budget cost, then propose permutation relations and a refined Markov random field to synthesize relational data. The primary arXiv abstract reports better aggregate-query accuracy than existing methods on real datasets and TPC-H. This is a targeted method-level response, not independent evidence that DP relational synthesis is solved; a common comparison must charge equal privacy budgets and report join-aggregate utility, schema/constraint validity, runtime, and small-data behavior. Only the primary abstract and bibliographic record of PrivPetal were checked in this batch, not its full experimental tables.

### 3. Practical and Asymptotically Optimal Quantization of High-Dimensional Vectors in Euclidean Space for Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search (arXiv:2409.09913v1; SIGMOD 2025)

[arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09913); [ACM DOI 10.1145/3725413](https://doi.org/10.1145/3725413); local PDF: `data/fulltexts/arxiv/2409.09913v1.pdf` (16 pages); extracted text: `data/fulltexts/text/2409.09913v1.txt`.

Extended RaBitQ adapts the estimator/code to 1–10 bits per dimension and stages distance evaluation: use a short high-order code first, then read remaining bits only for candidates not ruled out by the error bound. It evaluates six public datasets, including 113.5 million 1,024-dimensional MS MARCO vectors, and reports favorable accuracy/runtime trade-offs against LVQ/SQ at matched memory. Its large-scale example uses 96 threads/48 cores for quantization; per-query ANN measurements are single-threaded. For the 100M-vector dataset it reports about two hours to quantize at 9 bits, while IVF construction takes more than a day in its setup. The paper scopes the demonstrated integration to in-memory, IVF-style search; efficient integration with graph indexes is left as future work. Results therefore depend on the index, hardware, bit budget, and recall target, rather than establish universal ANN superiority.

The later MRQ paper (PVLDB 2026) is a useful independent systems comparator: it combines PCA projection with projected/residual distance estimates, evaluates IVF and HNSW against RaBitQ on eight datasets, and reports up to 3.4× faster search with one-sixth the bits on OpenAI-3072. The result is not uniform: the paper says HNSW is faster on OpenAI-1536/3072 at equivalent recall, while MRQ tends to lead elsewhere; its stated baselines, projection, and index differ from the sampled study. The appropriate open test is a common, reproducible workload with end-to-end build cost, memory, recall, QPS, and index family held explicit.

## Bounded findings

1. **Differential-oblivious query support has advanced from isolated operators to some composed static queries, but not to a maintained end-to-end database.** Adore demonstrates bounded operator speedups and explicitly leaves realistic operator composition open; Doquet supplies select-join composition and stronger private-memory observation coverage, while leaving static-data updates and privacy-budget/index selection unresolved. A falsifiable systems study should compare operator plans under a fixed total (ε,δ) budget and explicit attacker view, then include update/maintenance costs and query-regression tails. It must not treat the papers' different threats and baselines as a direct speedup contest.

2. **Relational synthetic-data utility under DP is constrained by privacy-budget allocation and sample size, not just model expressiveness.** PrivLava's latent foreign-key model improves selected aggregate queries but reports noise-dominated behavior when data are small and high one-time cost; PrivPetal specifically targets the repeated-EM privacy cost. A matched evaluation can test whether the permutation-relation design improves join-aggregate utility at fixed privacy loss, especially as the number of entities, FK fan-out, and sample size vary. Novelty and universal superiority are not established here.

3. **Vector-quantization wins are conditional on the downstream index and the complete memory/recall budget.** Extended RaBitQ makes moderate compression with error bounds practical in its IVF workload; MRQ's later projection-based system shows a different favorable trade-off, while its own HNSW comparison reverses on two OpenAI embedding datasets. A falsifiable comparison should fix dataset snapshots, hardware, index family/search budget, recall target, and include index build plus vector access costs. Estimator error bounds alone do not predict end-to-end nearest-neighbor search ranking.

## Scope and limitations

These are three lexical-priority records from one frozen cell, not a representative estimate of database research. Follow-up checks were targeted: Doquet's official PVLDB full text and MRQ's official PVLDB paper were read for comparison boundaries; PrivPetal's primary arXiv abstract/publication details were checked but its full tables/code were not audited. No theorem was re-proved, SGX/privacy experiment rerun, DP method replicated, or ANN benchmark reproduced. These are bounded evidence-backed research leads, not claims of novelty or prevalence across the 198,690-candidate corpus.
