# Full-text audit batch 56 — software testing, lexical-priority 2020–2024

This batch closes the three lexical-priority records selected for `software_testing × 2020–2024` in the frozen frame. The period is arXiv first-submission period, not necessarily venue year. The PDFs were downloaded from arXiv and text-extracted locally; sampled-paper IDs, period, topic, and arm were checked against `data/fulltext_stratified_sample.jsonl`.

## 1. FuzzSplore: Visualizing Feedback-Driven Fuzzing Techniques (`2102.02527v2`)

The paper proposes an AFL++-based, analyst-driven dashboard for comparing fuzzing configurations on one target. Its four linked views are a t-SNE testcase scatterplot, edge-coverage-over-time plot, interesting-testcase plot, and testcase-generation graph; an analyst may use them to allocate CPUs, drop configurations, or tune fuzzers during a campaign. The paper identifies why scalar coverage or bug counts can hide target-specific behavior, and it acknowledges that bug discovery can be noisy and overfit to targets.

The evidence is an implementation and illustrative demonstration, not a controlled usability or effectiveness study: no recruited analyst sample, task-completion measure, or downstream bug-yield comparison is reported. t-SNE is stochastic and its layout is meaningful only after jointly processing the input set; the tool is AFL++-oriented, and visual patterns still require human interpretation. The paper is an arXiv preprint (v2 dated 2021-02-06); no peer-reviewed venue was verified from the record.

Later primary-source context: a 2026 TOSEM study elicited 120 visualization tasks from interviews with 33 fuzzing practitioners and assessed six existing tools; its taxonomy found that those tools collectively support only 10 of 120 tasks, and it lists FuzzSplore as supporting five task IDs. This is a task-coverage audit, not evidence that FuzzSplore fails at the five tasks it does support or that its users make worse decisions.

## 2. A Probabilistic Framework for Mutation Testing in Deep Neural Networks (`2208.06018v1`)

The authors argue that a binary mutation-killed decision can fluctuate with which stochastic DNN training instances are sampled. Their probabilistic mutation-testing (PMT) procedure estimates a posterior over outcomes and reports effect/similarity information instead of forcing every operator into a deterministic killed/not-killed label. The study reuses three model/dataset families (MNIST, MovieLens, UnityEyes) and eight mutation operators, reports 200 trained instances in the main analysis and 100 bootstrap replicates, and examines approximation error versus sample size. The authors report roughly one minute per mutation after model instances have already been trained, with parallelism.

Important qualifications from the paper itself: only three model/dataset settings were used to control computation and preserve comparison with DeepCrime; selected operators were chosen partly using earlier killability/triviality analyses; the absolute similarity-ratio thresholds are an empirical, user-facing scale, and the paper says intermediate threshold bands are somewhat arbitrary. The authors acknowledge empirical approximation error and that mutation validity itself remains debated. PMT makes the decision more graded/consistent in its tested setup; it does not establish that a PMT-killed mutation predicts a real field defect or that its thresholds are calibrated across models.

Venue verified: *Information and Software Technology* 155 (2023), article 107129, DOI 10.1016/j.infsof.2022.107129. Related but non-equivalent primary work includes a 2024 real-fault-derived mutation pipeline for deep reinforcement learning (a different learning regime) and a 2025 technical report that repairs a monotonicity defect in the earlier DeepCrime statistical killing criterion; neither is treated here as a direct validation of PMT's real-fault predictive validity.

## 3. Selecting Initial Seeds for Better JVM Fuzzing (`2408.08515v1`)

This ICSE 2025 paper studies 10 seed-selection methods, including control-flow-graph features (FISSCFG), on three JVMs (HotSpot, OpenJ9, Bisheng JDK) and two fuzzers (JavaTailor and VECT). It reports five repeated runs, generally 24 hours per run. On the two historical benchmark corpora, FISSCFG's selected subsets produced 1.42–2.69× the full-set count of unique inconsistencies in the reported comparisons, with about 30 seconds selection overhead; at a 50% seed budget it performed strongly across the tested budget range. In a separately collected GitHub corpus, other methods sometimes complement it, so CFG selection is not uniformly best at every corpus/budget.

The study also moves beyond discrepancy counts: after manual investigation and developer submission, it reports 25 previously unknown bugs, of which 21 were marked “confirmed” or “fixed” by developers; seven of those were detectable only with programs from the GitHub corpus. Do not read “21 confirmed or fixed” as 21 fixed, nor treat all discrepancies as bugs. External validity remains limited to three JVM implementations, two seed-driven fuzzers, and the chosen corpora and runtime. The authors explicitly note that 24 hours is shorter than industrial fuzzing, that randomness affects results, and that the seed methods have not been evaluated on every JVM fuzzer.

Venue verified: ICSE 2025, pp. 2012–2023. The evidence is stronger for real bug relevance than proxy-only fuzzing studies, but it remains one bounded experiment and not independent reproduction.

## Bounded findings

1. **Tool-evaluation endpoints form a ladder, and evidence at one level does not establish the next.** FuzzSplore demonstrates an interactive diagnostic interface but reports no analyst-outcome study. PMT improves the stability and granularity of mutation decisions in three DNN settings, but does not validate those decisions against real deployed faults. The JVM seed-selection study measures unique inconsistencies and separately reports developer disposition of 25 bugs, with 21 confirmed or fixed. A useful falsifiable study would test whether visual/task-level signals and mutation scores predict confirmed, deduplicated defects under equal compute budgets across targets. The three papers use different systems and outcomes, so this batch does not estimate a cross-field correlation or claim that any proxy is generally invalid.

2. **Mutation-test consistency is distinct from mutation realism and criterion validity.** PMT addresses instability from stochastic training instances and adds graded evidence, but its chosen thresholds and tested models/operators bound the claim. Later adjacent work derives mutation operators from real DRL faults, while a 2025 report addresses monotonicity in the earlier DeepCrime criterion. These advance different dimensions and do not establish that PMT's score predicts real-world DNN defects. A falsifiable follow-up would calibrate PMT decisions against a preregistered set of real DNN defects, test-set families, and training seeds, reporting false-positive/false-negative rates and cost at fixed budgets.

3. **For the evaluated JVM task, CFG-informed seed reduction is a promising budgeted strategy, not a universal seed-selection rule.** Across the paper's stated JVMs/fuzzers/corpora, FISSCFG improves reported inconsistency yield at tested budgets and has low feature-extraction overhead; a separate developer-triaged result includes 25 new bugs (21 confirmed or fixed). However, the open-source corpus can contribute bugs not found by the benchmark corpora, and other selectors sometimes lead on particular corpus/budget rows. A falsifiable replication would hold total preprocessing plus fuzzing cost fixed, vary JVM/fuzzer/version and corpus, preregister bug deduplication and developer-confirmation handling, and test whether the best seed features transfer.

## Scope and limitations

These are three full-text audits from one sampled topic-period cell, not a representative estimate for all software testing. The 2026 fuzzing-visualization taxonomy, 2024 real-fault DRL mutation paper, and 2025 DNN statistical-mutation report were checked as targeted follow-ups/context only; their full proofs/data/artifacts were not re-audited in this batch. No dashboard usability experiment, PMT reproduction, JVM fuzzing run, or novelty search over all relevant literature was performed. Findings are testable research leads, not novelty, prevalence, or causal claims.
