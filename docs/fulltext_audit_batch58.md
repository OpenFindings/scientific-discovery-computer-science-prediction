# Full-text audit batch 58 — compilers/programming languages, lexical-priority 2010–2014

This batch audits the three selected records in the frozen `compilers_programming_languages × 2010–2014 × lexical_priority` cell. The period is arXiv first-submission period, not necessarily the date the research was done or published. `1108.0476v5` is also listed in another screening topic; this audit is counted here under the compiler/programming-languages cell. All three sampled PDFs were downloaded, text-extracted, and checked against the frozen sample record.

## 1. Specifying and Staging Mixed-Initiative Dialogs with Program Generation and Transformation (`1108.0476v5`)

This paper gives a programming-language-inspired notation for mixed-initiative dialog episodes, argues that partial evaluation can stage implementations, and describes Scheme prototypes for mining compact specifications and generating executable “stagers.” Its Section 3.2 “Evaluation” is a combinatorial/formal analysis of how many dialog specifications fit the notation, not a user study or a measurement of task completion, error rate, or authoring effort. Sections 5.1 and 5.6 call the toolkit a proof-of-concept prototype and specify its Scheme/DrRacket execution environment. The paper therefore supports representational expressiveness and implementation feasibility, but not user-facing effectiveness.

Important counterevidence to treating this as an unchanged, untested idea is the targeted 2025 journal follow-up by Rowland and Perugini. That work adds concurrent/coroutine-like subdialogs, formalizes simplification/staging semantics, implements them in Haskell, and models parts of an online food-ordering system. It reports higher expression compression and compares the DSL conceptually with VoiceXML and State Chart XML. This is a substantive case study and implementation advance, although the reported case study/compression evidence is not a controlled comparison of user task success, time, errors, or independent authoring cost. The bounded opportunity is to test whether concision and formal implementability translate into better authoring and end-user outcomes on independently specified tasks.

## 2. Typing Context-Dependent Behavioural Variation (`1302.6332v1`)

The six-page PLACES/EPTCS paper defines ContextML, operational semantics for explicit context stacks and dispatch, and an annotated type system. Its progress and subject-reduction lemmas support the stated guarantee that a well-typed expression will not become stuck because no layer handles a dispatch. This guarantee is scoped to the formal model: context is explicit and layer activation follows program execution. The authors identify asynchronous evolution of the environment and event-driven context changes as future work.

A targeted 2012 COORDINATION follow-up by the same group is meaningful counterevidence to a broad “dynamic context is ignored” claim. It extends ContextML with resource policies, message passing, effects, and model checking; context changes may be triggered by program actions and exchanged messages. However, it describes an abstract bus and a formal/static verification method, not a deployed asynchronous runtime or an empirical evaluation. Its conclusion explicitly leaves a more powerful coordination model with concurrency, distribution, and asynchrony for future work. A useful next experiment is therefore not to repeat the type-safety theorem, but to measure whether such guarantees survive a concrete event-driven runtime and what latency/precision costs arise under adversarial context changes.

## 3. The Complexity of Flow Analysis in Higher-Order Languages (`1311.4733v1`)

Although this record entered arXiv in November 2013 and therefore falls in the frozen arXiv-submission stratum, its PDF is a Brandeis PhD dissertation dated August 2009. This is a discovered chronology mismatch: do not interpret it as a 2013-origin research result. The dissertation proves 0CFA decision PTIME-complete and, for the specified higher-order functional-language formulation, kCFA EXPTIME-complete for every fixed `k > 0`; it also characterizes exact kCFA as PTIME-complete. The preface explicitly says to expect proofs and theorems, not benchmarks or prototype implementations. These are formal worst-case decision-complexity results, not a claim that every practical workload necessarily exhibits exponential runtime.

The targeted PLDI 2010 follow-up “Resolving and Exploiting the k-CFA Paradox” is strong scope-correcting evidence: it explains why an identically named/formally related k-CFA is polynomial-time for object-oriented programs yet exponential-time for functional programs, due to object/closure representation, and derives a polynomial-time `m`-CFA hierarchy for functional programs. The paper also reports experiments in which m-CFA appears as precise as k-CFA at lower cost on its evaluated cases. Thus the theorem is model- and representation-specific, and there is prior empirical work on alternatives. A remaining, testable opportunity is a modern, matched replication across functional and OO encodings, separating worst-case complexity stressors from representative programs and measuring precision, time, and memory under clearly specified closure abstractions.

## Bounded findings

1. **Dialog-language compression is not yet task-level effectiveness evidence.** The 2011 sampled paper analyzes expressiveness and presents a Scheme proof of concept; the 2025 journal follow-up adds formal semantics, Haskell implementation, and an online-food-order case study with reported representation compression. Neither result, as described in the audited sources, establishes that independent developers author dialogs faster or that users complete tasks more accurately. Test DSL, VoiceXML, and SCXML on preregistered dialog tasks with independent authors and users; measure specification size, authoring time, completion, repair/error rate, and maintenance. The lead weakens if the DSL’s compression advantage does not persist under independently authored tasks or yields no author/user benefit. This is a bounded measurement gap, not a claim that the DSL lacks practical value.

2. **Static context-safety proofs need a runtime boundary test.** ContextML proves dispatch progress for well-typed programs in an explicit-context semantics; a related security extension models policy/resource behavior and message-driven context changes, but abstracts its communication bus and leaves concurrency/distribution/asynchrony as future work. The opportunity is to implement the verified semantics in an event-driven runtime and inject reordered, delayed, and concurrent context changes; compare static predictions with runtime violations, false alarms, and overhead. The claim weakens if the existing model already captures the tested event patterns with negligible cost and no soundness gap. This does not imply that the original formal guarantees are incorrect within their model.

3. **kCFA complexity claims must be reported with language and representation.** The sampled 2009 dissertation proves a worst-case EXPTIME-completeness result for kCFA (`k > 0`) in its functional higher-order formulation, while the PLDI 2010 follow-up explains a polynomial OO case and proposes polynomial m-CFA for functional programs, with lower-cost/competitive-precision results on its own experiments. A reproducible modern comparison should pin each analysis definition, language encoding, closure representation, benchmark set, precision metric, and compute budget, then include both worst-case-generated and real-program inputs. This may confirm or revise practical tradeoffs; it cannot overturn a valid formal worst-case theorem merely by finding easy instances. The arXiv submission date is not the dissertation’s research date.

## Scope and limitations

This is a complete audit of three sampled records from one lexical-priority topic-period cell plus three targeted, full-text follow-ups. The 2025 dialog study, 2012 secure-context paper, and PLDI 2010 k-CFA paper are context/follow-up evidence, not additional records in the frozen sample. No user study, ContextML runtime, or static-analysis benchmark was run in this batch. Findings are bounded, falsifiable research leads—not novelty, prevalence, causal, or field-wide consensus claims. The 2009 dissertation versus 2013 arXiv date mismatch is retained as an explicit sampling/chronology caveat.
