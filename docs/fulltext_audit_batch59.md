# Full-text audit batch 59 — agent memory/coordination, lexical-priority 2010–2014

This batch audits the three selected records in `agent_memory_coordination × 2010–2014 × lexical_priority`. The stratum is based on arXiv first-submission date, not original publication date. That distinction materially changes the historical reading of two papers here: their PDFs identify them as *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* articles from 2000 and 2002, although their arXiv submissions were in 2011. The third is a 2013 journal paper. All three selected PDFs were downloaded, extracted, and checked against the saved frame.

## 1. Robust Agent Teams via Socially-Attentive Monitoring (`1106.0235v1`)

The PDF identifies this as a JAIR paper, volume 12 (2000), pages 105–147—not a paper originating in 2011. It studies the “monitoring selectivity” problem for detecting broken social/team-plan relationships. The formal result is conditional: for simple teams and the paper’s observability/plan assumptions, distributed monitoring can be both sound and complete while monitoring key agents; the centralized approach may need to monitor all teammates and trades soundness for completeness or vice versa. The full article includes a monitoring framework and empirical investigations in ModSAF and RoboCup-related settings, including online/offline diagnosis and team-quality feedback. It does not establish a universal monitoring policy for arbitrary heterogeneous modern teams; the theorem depends on its team/observability assumptions, and the paper itself leaves the relation between its team-quality feedback and task performance open.

## 2. Towards Adjustable Autonomy for the Real World (`1106.4573v1`)

This PDF is a JAIR article, volume 17 (2002), pages 171–228, originally submitted/published in 2002, despite its 2011 arXiv posting. Its central design is a conditional transfer-of-control strategy: an agent can transfer a decision to a human or agent and change team coordination constraints while waiting, so it can balance individual decision quality against delay and team miscoordination. The evaluation is unusually concrete for this early literature: E-Elves ran for ten research-group users from June through December 2000, with roughly 5–10 Friday agents per user and nearly 700 monitored meetings. The paper reports daily coordination activity, meeting delays/autonomous delays, and whether observed strategy behavior matched model predictions. This demonstrates actual deployment/use and strategy diversity, but not a randomized causal comparison of user outcomes against a baseline. Some additional auction evidence is only three logged dates; do not conflate it with the much larger meeting-use period.

The targeted AAMAS 2009 follow-up, “Improving Adjustable Autonomy Strategies for Time-Critical Domains,” extends the approach to continuous-time policies, interruptible actions, and explicit resolution of human/agent decision inconsistencies in a disaster-response simulation. It reports 50-run outcome averages for several response-time distributions and statistical significance in one comparison. Crucially, it also reports that the RIAACT advantage depends on the probability that resolving a detected inconsistency will actually improve the team decision: when this probability is low (0.3 in the reported setting), RIAACT can underperform “Always Accept.” This is substantive progress and counterevidence to any claim that the 2002 approach was never tested, but it makes model calibration and domain-specific outcomes central.

## 3. Modification of Contract Net Protocol (CNP): A Rule-Updation Approach (`1312.4259v1`)

The paper proposes updated-CNP, allowing the manager to change an already allocated task before the contractor’s final report, rather than restarting conventional CNP. It implements a predator–prey case study in JADE/FIPA-ACL and JATLite/KQML. The reported comparison uses five tasks, with two task changes during execution, and plots task updates, repetitions, and elapsed-time/coordination outcomes. The authors describe near-immediate accommodation of most changes, but the experiment does not establish performance over broad workloads or real application task distributions; the paper’s own future work calls for application-specific studies and further work on scalability, load balance, fairness, robustness, and other limits. No independent replication of this exact in-flight task-change mechanism was verified in this batch.

A targeted 2019 journal paper, “Task Assignment of the Improved Contract Net Protocol under a Multi-Agent System,” is relevant adjacent evidence, not a replication of updated-CNP. It compares a different pheromone/response-threshold ICNP with classical and dynamic CNP in Repast simulations. The authors vary tasks (50–1,000) and contractor counts (10–50); for 30 agents, 1,000 tasks and maximum load 3, they report 133 errors/86.7% completion for classical CNP, 68/93.2% for dynamic CNP, and 11 successfully re-forwarded error tasks/100% completion for their ICNP. It shows that other CNP modifications have been evaluated at larger synthetic scale, while leaving the 2013 paper’s particular in-flight task-modification rule unvalidated. The 2019 authors also note that redundant public-environment information can reduce contractor efficiency and identify collaborative bidding/task assignment as future work.

## Bounded findings

1. **ArXiv submission-year strata can contain much older research.** Two of the three sampled records in this one 2010–2014 arXiv cell are visibly JAIR articles from 2000 and 2002. Thus an arXiv-year trend in this small cell would mix repository-deposit date with publication/research date. Reconcile venue/publication year and earliest public version before making historical trend claims. This is a demonstrated sampling caveat for this cell, not an estimate of how often the problem occurs across the corpus.

2. **Selective monitoring has a strong conditional result, not a universal guarantee.** The 2000 paper’s formal analysis supports sound-and-complete distributed detection while monitoring only key agents under its simple-team and observability assumptions, with experiments in two multi-agent domains. This is positive evidence that all-to-all monitoring is not always necessary. A modern test should vary partial observability, team hierarchy, agent failures, communication loss, and key-agent availability, measuring false alarms, missed failures, messages, and task completion. The claim weakens if soundness/completeness collapses under realistic variants or if the communication savings vanish; the theorem remains valid within its stated model.

3. **Adjustable-autonomy benefit depends on both coordination design and calibration of when human input helps.** The 2002 E-Elves deployment documents seven months of use by ten people and roughly 700 meetings; the 2009 disaster-response follow-up adds continuous-time planning and interruptible resolution. Yet the latter explicitly reports a condition under which RIAACT is worse than a simpler baseline when the estimated usefulness of resolving disagreement is low. A falsifiable follow-up should prospectively calibrate this probability and compare full transfer strategies with “always autonomous,” “always accept,” and simple timeout policies across task deadlines, human response distributions, and real user outcomes. These results do not imply the 2002 deployment established causal user benefit or that RIAACT is uniformly superior.

4. **CNP results need to be compared by the actual protocol change and workload, not by the shared “improved CNP” label.** The 2013 sampled paper demonstrates in-flight task changes on a five-task predator–prey case; the 2019 adjacent ICNP reports larger synthetic task-assignment experiments and higher completion under its own pheromone/threshold/re-forwarding mechanism. That later evidence does not validate the 2013 rule. A direct test should implement both mechanisms and classical/DCNP on versioned dynamic workloads, vary task arrivals and changes, report messages/bytes, wall time, retries, failure recovery, task utility and fairness across seeds, and release the workload and code. The 2013 claim strengthens only if its rule improves these endpoints beyond existing variants at matched conditions.

## Scope and limitations

This is a full-text audit of three records from one lexical-priority topic-period cell plus two targeted primary-source full-text follow-ups. The 2009 RIAACT and 2019 ICNP papers are contextual/adjacent sources, not members of the frozen sample. No monitor, MDP policy, or CNP variant was reimplemented. The cell is not a prevalence estimate; two arXiv records have substantial publication-date offsets, so arXiv period must not be interpreted as publication chronology.
