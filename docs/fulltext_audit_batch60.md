# Full-text audit batch 60 — agent reliability/security, lexical-priority 2010–2014

This batch audits three sampled records from `agent_reliability_security × 2010–2014 × lexical_priority`. The stratum refers to arXiv first-submission year, not original publication year. Two PDFs identify earlier journal publications (2002 and 2011). All three PDFs were downloaded, text-extracted, and reviewed against their title pages, formal claims, evaluation sections, and conclusions.

## 1. Competitive Safety Analysis: Robust Decision-Making in Multi-Agent Systems (`1106.4570v1`)

The arXiv copy is a 2011 deposit of Moshe Tennenholtz’s JAIR 17 (2002), pp. 363–378 article. The paper defines safety-level strategies and asks when their worst-case payoff compares favorably with a Nash-equilibrium payoff. Its equal-payoff results are conditional: in generic non-reducible 2×2 games, equality is shown when the safety-level strategy is strictly mixed; the paper explicitly warns that it need not hold for a pure safety-level strategy. In the extended decentralized load-balancing model, it proves a 9/8-competitive safety strategy in the many-player limit; other ratios depend on the network structure and assumptions. These are mathematical guarantees in specified games, not empirical evidence that deployed agents achieve the same ratios under model error, repeated interaction, or communication failures.

## 2. Interactive POMDP Lite (`1304.5159v1`)

The paper appeared in IJCAI 2013, pp. 2298–2305. It proposes nested MDP for fully observable interaction and I-POMDP Lite for partially observable stochastic games, with a structural assumption about the other agent’s policy. Theorem 4 bounds policy loss linearly in the error of predicted opponent strategy, with additional factors involving the action/observation spaces, reward scale, and discounting. This is a useful conditional guarantee: prediction accuracy is an input to the bound, not something the theorem guarantees the method will attain. The paper evaluates an autonomous-vehicle intersection scenario and a series of synthetic POSGs; the intersection evaluation runs 800 episodes against a scripted human-driver model, without human participants. Its own discussion describes limitations in approximate-policy comparisons and the assumptions about sensing and opponent behavior. Thus it is evidence for an analyzable intention-aware planning approach, not proof of real-world human-driver safety or broad superiority under arbitrary opponent misspecification.

## 3. Drake: An Efficient Executive for Temporal Plans with Choice (`1401.4606v1`)

The arXiv copy was first deposited in 2014, but its front matter identifies a JAIR 42 (2011), pp. 607–659 article. Drake compiles labeled temporal networks into a compact representation to reduce storage while supporting dispatch. Evaluation uses 100 randomly generated structured problems at each of several choice sizes, 2–11 binary or 2–7 ternary choices, up to roughly 2,000 consistent component STNs, and a Lisp implementation on a 2.66 GHz machine with 4 GB RAM. The paper reports up to about 700× smaller compiled representations for the largest cases, with many cases around 1–10 kB. The trade-off is substantial variability: some compilation cases take hours and are up to 100× slower; a few dispatch-latency outliers reach tens of seconds. The authors caution that performance on a particular application may be better or worse depending on plan structure. These results support a structural memory/latency trade-off, not a blanket claim of real-time suitability.

## Bounded findings

1. **ArXiv-year sampling is not publication chronology.** Two of these three sampled records are earlier JAIR articles (2002 and 2011) posted to arXiv in 2011 and 2014. The third is an IJCAI 2013 paper posted in 2013. This cell demonstrates that repository-deposit strata can mix publication eras; it does not estimate how frequent this is in the corpus. Historical trend analysis must reconcile publication venue/year and first public version.

2. **Opponent-prediction guarantees are conditional and motivate calibration tests.** I-POMDP Lite’s theorem bounds policy loss in terms of opponent-strategy prediction error, while the experiments use modeled/synthetic opponents, including a scripted traffic agent over 800 episodes. The guarantee does not establish that the prediction error is small for real human drivers. A falsifiable follow-up should compare predicted-opponent policy, robust/worst-case planning, and simple baselines against held-out human and synthetic behavior while measuring calibration error, collision/near-miss rates, delay, and reward. The practical lead weakens if prediction error is poorly calibrated or improvements vanish against shifted opponent populations; it strengthens if the bound and empirical loss track one another on held-out settings.

3. **Compact plan compilation trades average space savings for structure-sensitive tails.** Drake reports very large memory reductions on structured random benchmarks, yet also hours-long compilation and tens-of-seconds dispatch outliers. A useful next experiment is to characterize which graph/choice features predict those tails, then benchmark matched plan families on current hardware with p50/p95/p99 compilation and dispatch latency, peak memory, and deadline misses. The deployment case weakens if realistic plans land in the tail or if tail predictors cannot be detected before execution; it strengthens if the compact cases cover target workloads and tail risks can be bounded.

4. **Competitive safety results should be reused only within their game assumptions.** The 9/8 load-balancing bound and the safety-level/Nash equalities are formal results for specified payoff/game families; strict mixing matters for the generic 2×2 equality, and the article says pure safety-level strategies need not satisfy it. A follow-up can perturb payoffs, permit correlated or repeated behavior, and introduce failures, then report the safety guarantee and equilibrium comparison separately. The lead weakens if the desired ratio depends on brittle assumptions; it strengthens if the ratio survives explicitly bounded perturbations. No new theorem or implementation was produced here.

## Scope and limitations

This is a full-text audit of three sampled articles in one cell, not a prevalence estimate or a full-text review of the 198,690-candidate pool. The findings are bounded to the cited article models and evaluations. No agent planner or temporal-network executive was reimplemented, and no human-subject or deployment evaluation was performed.
