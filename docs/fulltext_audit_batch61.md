# Full-text audit batch 61 — agent memory/coordination, lexical-priority 2020–2024

This batch follows the lexical-priority arm in `agent_memory_coordination × 2020–2024`. All three selected base IDs now have full-text audits. For `2305.15797`, I retrieved and audited the 12-page v1 PDF; the later v2 is withdrawn and has no PDF. The official withdrawal comment says equations 4–6 were not fully correctly derived and underpin the marginal-based framework. Accordingly, v1 is documented as what the authors claimed in that version, but it is not treated as currently validated positive evidence. I also inspected two primary-source follow-ups: a distinct 2023 cooperative-estimation paper by the same authors, and a 2026 AAAI theory/benchmark study of non-monotonic value factorization. These are contextual evidence, not replacements or independent replications of the sampled methods.

## 1. NQMIX: Non-monotonic Value Function Factorization (`2104.01939v4`)

NQMIX combines QMIX-style local utilities with an actor/off-policy policy-gradient component and an unconstrained MLP mixer, aiming to remove QMIX's monotonicity restriction. In SMAC, it reports win-rate curves on three maps, with three independent runs per method, and reports NQMIX beating QMIX and COMA on the more complex heterogeneous-unit maps while landing between them on a simpler map. Its ablation suggests the MLP mixer matters, but the ablation results also vary by map. This is encouraging but narrow evidence: the main comparison uses only three selected maps and three seeds; the authors explicitly identify evaluation on additional maps and possible actor bottlenecks as future work. The paper's stated representation and continuous-action ambitions are not validated by those SMAC plots.

## 2. Greedy-based Value Representation for Optimal Coordination (`2211.12075v1`)

GVR addresses relative overgeneralization by modifying targets for inferior joint actions (inferior target shaping) and replaying superior experiences, seeking to make the globally optimal joint action the unique stable/self-transition node under stated conditions. The paper supports its mechanism with matrix games, predator–prey reward-shaping tasks, and SMAC; its SMAC experiments use five seeds and train for 2–5 million steps depending on map. The appendix reports conditions where the exploration threshold needed to eliminate non-optimal self-transition nodes grows toward 1 as the number of agents and individual action choices increase, and says the resulting large-exploration requirement is inapplicable to long-episode tasks. The authors also report that the optional optimality/stability trade-off can hurt on some difficult tasks because its critics conflict. Thus GVR is not evidence that target shaping/replay uniformly solves coordination: action-space exploration and critic stability remain central conditions.

## 3. Versioned audit: distributed set-membership filtering (`2305.15797v1`; v2 withdrawn)

The selected record's v1 PDF is available (12 pages) and was reviewed locally. It develops two distributed set-membership-filtering frameworks using absolute and neighbor-relative measurements, proves outer-bounding relations to projections of a centralized uncertain range, and reports linear/nonlinear simulated examples. The later arXiv version v2 is withdrawn; the current arXiv record has no v2 PDF, and the authors say the derivation of equations 4–6 was not fully correct and that these equations underpin the marginal-based framework. I preserve the v1 claims and its simulations as version-specific evidence, but do not present the withdrawn derivation as validated or assume v1's theorem remains sound after the authors' notice. The withdrawal comment is the authors' own correction signal, not an independent mathematical proof of the error.

As nearby context, the same authors' separate 2023 paper, *Set-Membership Filtering-Based Cooperative State Estimation for Multi-Agent Systems* (`2305.10366`, accepted by the Chinese Control Conference according to its arXiv comments), is available as a seven-page primary-source PDF. It develops centralized and distributed constrained-zonotope estimators using local absolute and relative measurements. Its illustrative simulation uses five UAVs in a 2-D model. The distributed/finite-horizon estimates contain the simulated true positions but are more conservative (larger uncertainty sets) than the standard centralized baseline; the authors describe complexity/accuracy trade-offs and leave nonlinear systems and stability for future work. This is related work, not evidence that the withdrawn `2305.15797` derivation was repaired or validated.

## 4. Targeted follow-up: non-monotonic value-factorization dynamics (AAAI 2026)

*Beyond Monotonicity: Revisiting Factorization Principles in Multi-Agent Q-Learning* studies an unconstrained, non-monotonic QMIX variant. It models training as a continuous-time gradient flow and proves a conditional stability result under approximately-greedy/softmax behavior: in its model, IGM-inconsistent zero-loss points are unstable saddles while IGM-consistent solutions are stable attractors. It evaluates matrix games, SMAC, and Google Research Football; benchmark win-rate figures report five independent runs. This supplies newer support for a route different from GVR's target-shaping and superior-replay mechanism, but it is not a direct reproduction of NQMIX or GVR. Crucially, the formal derivation is for a single-state game with a unique global optimum and specified smooth/non-degenerate value representation; its dynamical proof does not establish unconditional convergence of neural agents in arbitrary partially observed, long-horizon MARL. The benchmark results are empirical support, not a general theorem about all tasks.

## Bounded findings

1. **“Remove monotonicity” is not one algorithm, and evidence depends on the learning/exploration mechanism.** NQMIX uses an actor/off-policy policy-gradient hybrid and reports three-run SMAC comparisons; GVR uses inferior-target shaping and superior replay, has five-seed SMAC runs plus constructed matrix games, and identifies a high-exploration scaling problem; the 2026 AAAI paper studies non-monotonic Q-learning with policy-coupled approximately-greedy exploration and a SARSA-style target. These results support a mechanism-sensitive research question, not a settled claim that unconstrained mixers always outperform QMIX. A falsifiable matched study should implement these mechanisms with matched network capacity, training compute, environments, seeds, and exploration budgets, then measure team return, optimal-joint-action rate, instability, and compute as team size/action count grow. The broad claim weakens if advantages disappear under matched resources or larger teams; it strengthens if mechanism-specific gains replicate across held-out tasks.

2. **Exploration scalability is a concrete stress point for GVR's optimal-consistency guarantee.** The GVR appendix derives an exploration threshold that approaches 1 as the number of agents and individual action choices increase, and explicitly warns that this is inapplicable to long-episode tasks; it also reports optional stability/optimality trade-off settings that underperform on some difficult benchmarks. Test the threshold against actual optimal-joint-action discovery, wall-clock/environment steps, and policy variance while scaling both team size and horizon. This finding would weaken if practical exploration schedules attain the guarantee with stable cost at larger sizes; it would strengthen if required exploration and instability rise together.

3. **A later withdrawal changes how the earlier full text can be used.** The audited v1 of `2305.15797` claims general uncertain-range derivations and distributed outer-bounding results, with linear/nonlinear simulations. The authors' later v2 withdrawal says equations 4–6—foundational to its marginal-based framework—were not fully correctly derived. A distinct same-author 2023 paper (`2305.10366`) demonstrates related centralized/distributed estimation on a five-UAV simulation but has more conservative sets than the centralized benchmark and does not by itself repair or validate the withdrawn derivation. Trace any corrected version, conference publication, and downstream citations, and keep v1 and v2 provenance separate. This is a source-integrity finding, not an independent proof of which equations are mathematically wrong.
