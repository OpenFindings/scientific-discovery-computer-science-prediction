## 1. Uncovering the Hidden Cost of Model Compression (`2308.14969v3`)

- **Question and protocol:** How do compressed pretrained vision models transfer when adapted by visual prompting, particularly with scarce target labels, and what happens to calibration? The paper covers eight image datasets, ResNet-18/34/50, CLIP ViT-L, DeiT-T and Swin-T; compares three visual-prompt label-mapping approaches and sparse/pruned and quantized checkpoints. Configurations use three random seeds; the authors report more than 15,000 experiments.
- **Evidence:** Dense models usually beat their sparse counterparts under visual prompting, with large dataset-dependent gaps. For compressed CLIP, 4× compression under ILM-VP loses about 45 percentage points on OxfordPets and 24 points on Caltech101, while GTSRB/EuroSAT gaps are small. Accuracy and data budget interact: sparse-model transfer can be particularly weak with very few target examples. Quantization is not cost-free either: 2-bit models can fall substantially below full precision, though 4-bit models often come closer and occasionally match.
- **Reliability distinction:** In the studied VP settings, increasing sparsity generally worsens calibration (ECE), whereas increased quantization tends to maintain or reduce ECE even while accuracy declines. Thus accuracy loss alone does not predict confidence-calibration behavior, and pruning and quantization should not be collapsed into a single “compression” treatment.
- **Boundaries:** This is a large experiment count but a specific family of vision checkpoints/adaptation protocols and benchmark datasets; three seeds are reported, and most conclusions emphasize ILM-VP. It does not establish a universal deployment-speed, energy, or quality law for compressed models, nor does it test current generative foundation models in ordinary fine-tuning/serving workflows.
- **Sections checked:** Abstract/introduction; experimental setup; sparse ResNet and compressed CLIP results; 2–4-bit quantized DeiT/Swin results; calibration/ECE analysis; cross-modal reprogramming tables; conclusion and supplementary results.
- **Version/status:** arXiv v3, dated 2024-03-15 in the PDF; first arXiv submission 2023-08-29, so this item is assigned to the frame by first-submission year. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14969).

## 2. Are Compressed Language Models Less Subgroup Robust? (`2403.17811v1`)

- **Question and protocol:** Does compression change worst-group accuracy on NLP datasets? The paper evaluates 18 compression methods/settings for BERT-family models on MultiNLI, CivilComments and SCOTUS, tracking mean and worst-group accuracy. The arXiv v1 is dated 2024-03, but the work appeared earlier at EMNLP 2023; retain the frame's arXiv-first-submission stratum while using the peer-reviewed venue for bibliographic status.
- **Evidence and counterevidence:** In MultiNLI and SCOTUS, smaller models often lose both average and worst-group accuracy, with exceptions. In CivilComments, most compressed models improve worst-group accuracy despite slight average-accuracy declines; even a very small BERT variant can beat the baseline worst-group score, which the authors hypothesize may reflect regularization against overfitting. Compression therefore does not consistently trade minority-group performance away to preserve the mean. Post-training dynamic/static quantization is generally less subgroup-robust than quantization-aware training in their setup; equal parameter counts can also have different group outcomes depending on method/initialization.
- **Boundaries:** Three English datasets, BERT variants, and compression procedures evaluated largely in isolation; the authors explicitly call for multilingual analysis and combinations of methods. This does not imply compression improves fairness generally: group definitions and outcomes are dataset-specific.
- **Sections checked:** Experimental setup and model/method inventory; dataset subgroup definitions; results by dataset, compression method and task complexity; conclusion and limitations.
- **Version/status:** arXiv v1 submitted 2024-03-26; peer-reviewed as EMNLP 2023, pages 15859–15868, DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.983. [ACL Anthology full paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.983/).

## 3. GraphFM: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Graph Foundation Model (`2406.08310v2`)

- **Question and protocol:** Do self-supervised graph representation methods generalize across downstream tasks, graph scales and training/early-stopping choices? The benchmark reimplements eight contrastive/generative graph self-supervised methods on six graph datasets: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Flickr, Reddit and ogbn-arxiv. It compares full-batch with node/subgraph mini-batch training, multiple downstream tasks and task-based stopping criteria. Full-batch results use five random seeds; the paper uses a common hyperparameter-search budget.
- **Evidence for conditional transfer:** On the small citation graphs, many node-classification-selected checkpoints still score well on link-prediction and clustering. On large graphs, results vary more by method, task and mini-batch strategy; generative methods do not uniformly dominate or underperform, and memory/throughput rankings also depend on training strategy. Selecting checkpoints by link prediction or clustering can improve that selected task (especially link prediction for contrastive methods), but does not yield one criterion that improves every downstream task.
- **Counterevidence and scope:** The result is not “graph SSL never generalizes”: the small-graph experiments show useful cross-task transfer. The larger-scale and stopping-criterion evidence is narrower: Reddit runs often exceeded 24 hours, so several experiments focus on Flickr and ogbn-arxiv; results cover eight methods and six conventional node-feature datasets, not textual-attribute or heterogeneous graphs. The paper itself calls for broader stopping criteria and extensions to textual graphs.
- **Sections checked:** Dataset/method selection and common tuning setup; RQ1–RQ4 protocols; node classification, cross-task, mini-batch, efficiency and alternative early-stopping results; future directions; appendix notes on runtime-driven dataset exclusions.
- **Version/status:** arXiv v2. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08310).

## Targeted full-text counterexample: Understanding the Effect of Model Compression on Social Bias in Large Language Models (EMNLP 2023)

Gonçalves and Strubell compare quantization and knowledge distillation across BERT, RoBERTa and Pythia model sizes (70M–6.9B parameters), using CrowS-Pairs, StereoSet and SEAT for gender, race and religion bias. Dynamic int8 quantization and distillation reduce several measured bias scores; for Pythia, the apparent quantization regularization effect places the best language-model/bias trade-off near 20% of the original pretraining steps. This is direct counterevidence to “compression always worsens every quality dimension,” but not proof of a general fairness improvement: outcomes vary by model/category/task, the benchmark covers only three identity dimensions and largely Western stereotypes, and the authors label the results preliminary and not production-ready. [ACL Anthology full text](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.161/).

## Cross-paper synthesis and tests

The combined evidence supports **conditional, metric-specific effects**, not “compression helps” or “compression hurts.” The transfer interface and target-data budget matter; pruning and quantization can behave differently; subgroup and calibration metrics can move differently from mean accuracy; and in graph SSL, the stopping objective can favor one downstream task over another. These results come from unrelated model/task families and must not be pooled as one effect size.

1. **Compression × adaptation × target-data budget:** Compare dense, pruned and quantized checkpoints with matched pretraining/backbones on visual prompting, linear probe, fine-tuning and cross-domain transfer at 1/2/5/full-shot budgets. Preregister accuracy, ECE, latency, energy and uncertainty intervals. The claimed interaction weakens if method/adaptation/budget interactions disappear on held-out datasets.
2. **Subgroup robustness:** On new multilingual datasets with prespecified label×attribute groups, compare dense, quantized, distilled, pruned and quantization-aware models at matched mean accuracy and size. Report group counts, mean/worst-group accuracy and calibration. The conditionality claim weakens if worst-group changes become consistently monotonic in size across methods and datasets.
3. **Graph checkpoint selection:** On held-out graph families, choose checkpoints using node classification, link prediction, clustering, a fixed multi-objective score, or a validation-only unsupervised criterion; evaluate every choice on all tasks. The “no single best stopping objective” claim weakens if one criterion consistently Pareto-dominates across graph sizes and task families.

No new experiment was run in this audit; it makes no novelty, prevalence, or production-readiness claim.
