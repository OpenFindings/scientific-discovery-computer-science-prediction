# Full-text audit batch 8 — latest-period random arm

Audit date: 2026-10-11. Selection: four papers chosen from the fixed uniform-random arm for the 2025–2026-through-10-11 submission stratum. Two additional primary papers were followed from the sampled agent-skills paper to check its empirical basis and later evidence. A random draw makes the source selection auditable; four papers are still far too few to estimate field-wide frequencies.

## 2512.23480 — Agentic AI for Autonomous Defense in Software Supply Chain Security

Sampled topic: software supply chain. Checked §§IV–VII and figures 2–4. The paper says it evaluated simulated and “real-world” GitHub Actions/Jenkins pipelines over four vulnerability classes, with rule, provenance, RL-only and commercial scanner baselines. It reports that its method wins on F1, autonomously executes over 90% of mitigations, and adds under 6% pipeline time.

Those are claims in the paper, but the reported empirical record is not sufficiently specified to independently audit effect size: the methods/results do not give a named repository/sample inventory, number of vulnerabilities or CI runs, train/test split, per-class confusion counts, uncertainty intervals, or a release link to code/data/trajectories. Figures 2–4 are plotted summaries without numerical tables or denominators. “Real-world” deployment is described, but no operational duration or deployment cohort is stated in the reviewed text. Treat this as an evidence-reporting limitation, not proof the system was not built or that the claims are false. Do not use the abstract's claimed advantage as a replicated result.

## 2503.09217 — Evaluating the Generalizability of LLMs in Automated Program Repair

Sampled topic: program repair/verification. Checked transformation setup, model/prompt protocol, Tables II–III, threats and future work. Eleven open models (6.7B–34B) were tested on the original Defects4J and a semantics-preserving transformed version. The mean number of correct fixes fell 49.48%; plausible fixes fell 42.90%. For the four models selected as weakest generalizers, adding repair-relevant input helped: bug reports yielded +111.72% correct and +97.77% plausible fixes on average relative to those models' transformed-set two-shot baseline. Still, only two of twelve tested model/prompt setups slightly exceeded their original Defects4J result; ten remained below.

This is a strong within-paper contrast, not proof that all APR systems fail under real-world distribution shift: the new set is transformed from the same 438 single-function Java bugs, only 11 open models were selected, and plausible-patch correctness required manual semantic review. The paper states those boundaries and releases data/code. Its strongest actionable implication is to test syntax-preserving transformations and report results with/without repair context, rather than to treat one benchmark score as generalization.

### Independent follow-up: 2604.21579 — A Metamorphic Testing Approach to Diagnosing Memorization in LLM-Based Program Repair

This follow-up tests seven models on transformed Defects4J and newer GitBug-Java, with 10 independent prompts per original/transformed bug. It finds statistically significant Defects4J drops for all seven models among bugs each model could solve originally (about 4.01–15.98 points), which converges with the sampled 2025 paper on transformation sensitivity. But the new study is a useful counterexample to a simplistic universal claim: GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 do not show significant average drops on the newer GitBug-Java sample; GPT-4o-mini's −8.67-point result is not conventionally significant (p=.074), although individual bugs still have large regressions. The smaller solvable-bug count limits power. For four open models, drops are largest among lower-NLL/familiar Defects4J bugs, but several models also show a second rise in high-NLL bins; NLL is a familiarity proxy, not proof of training leakage. This narrows the synthesis: representation sensitivity recurs, but its magnitude depends on benchmark vintage, solvability and model, and it cannot by itself diagnose memorization.

## 2602.20867 — SoK: Agentic Skills: Beyond Tool Use in LLM Agents

Sampled topic: agent reliability/security. Checked its survey method, taxonomy claims, and benchmark evidence. This is a useful map of a fast-moving topic, not an independent skill-efficacy experiment. The authors describe a 2020–Feb-2025 search and an in-depth review subset; the taxonomy is a synthesis, not externally validated. Its cited early SkillsBench result is now superseded by a later SkillsBench version; use the primary benchmark's current version rather than repeating the SoK's old count/effect size.

## Citation follow-up: 2602.12670v4 — SkillsBench

The current arXiv version reports 87 tasks across eight domains, 18 model–harness configurations, matched no-skill/curated-skill conditions, and three selected trials per cell. Mean task-macro pass rate rises from 33.9% to 50.5% (+16.6 percentage points); every evaluated configuration rises, but the gain ranges from +4.1 to +25.7 points. Importantly, 13/87 tasks have negative deltas. The authors' paired-trajectory audit attributes failure cases to overly heavy workflows, displacement of a stronger default, or tools the agent cannot debug. Tested benchmark skills were selected from the top quality quartile; authors explicitly call this an optimistic benchmark-to-ecosystem gap. This is substantial primary evidence for benefit under curated conditions, alongside direct counterevidence to “skills always help.”

## Citation follow-up: 2606.10546v1 — SkillAxe

SkillAxe studies evaluation-guided refinement of LLM-authored skills. Its SkillsBench experiment uses a different/older task snapshot (77 tasks yielding results), one Claude Opus 4.5 + Harbor setup, two trials per task, and both native test and LLM fair-grader evaluations. Under its native method-D scoring, overall pass rate rises 26.7% to 41.6%, but the conditional quality among tasks with evaluable outputs is identical (57.1%): the gain is coverage/completion, not better answers conditional on producing output. Under a fair grader it reports 42.7% no-skill vs 54.5% SkillAxe; human skills reach 62.3%. In a separate SpreadsheetBench held-out set of 50 common-completion tasks, SkillAxe's 22-skill library and the 69-skill LLM-self library both score 52% (vs 16% no-skill), so the advantage there is library compression/activation rather than accuracy. Fair grading/compliance use LLM judges, and the paper acknowledges judge bias, skill interactions, and remaining gap to humans. Because the benchmark version, harness, models, and scoring differ from SkillsBench v4, these numbers are complementary mechanism evidence, not a head-to-head replication.

## 2506.02718 — End-to-End Optimization of LLM-Driven Multi-Agent Search Systems

Sampled topic: agent coordination. Checked benchmarks, main table, ablations and limitations. MHGPO reports improved multi-hop QA results for a three-agent Rewriter→Reranker→Answerer pipeline using Llama 3.1 8B, including a HotpotQA F1 increase from 21.45 for the unoptimized MAS to 50.86 for a MHGPO variant. The evaluated domains are HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA and MuSiQue, with static iterative retrieval. The paper explicitly limits the claim: QA-only rather than open-ended search or long-horizon tool use; up to 8B models; fixed three-agent topology; broader dynamic environments and larger/different teams remain untested. So this is promising evidence for this specific retrieval-QA stack, not yet for autonomous research agents generally.

## Cross-paper synthesis (bounded)

In this latest-period draw, the most solid recurring lesson is about *where* gains come from and when they transfer: procedural aids can make more runs complete, but they do not automatically improve the correctness of completed work; improvements are conditional on artifact quality, model/harness, task, and evaluation protocol. A useful next experiment is a preregistered paired benchmark that reports (i) completion/coverage separately from correctness conditional on completion, (ii) per-task negative effects, (iii) expert-curated vs refined/self-authored skill arms, and (iv) multiple harnesses and realistic ecosystem-quality skills. The APR cross-paper audit separately finds converging transformation sensitivity but a meaningful newer-benchmark/model counterexample to uniform average drops; this is recorded in the earlier cross-period finding 006. These are candidate syntheses, not claims of global novelty or population-level prevalence.
