# Full-text audit batch 9 — filling the 2015–2019 random-arm gap

Audit date: 2026-10-11. Four papers were selected from the already-fixed uniform-random arm of the 2015–2019 submission stratum, in four topics. This is a coverage repair, not a claim that the sample is representative enough for prevalence estimates.

## 1905.11614v3 — Uncertainty-based Continual Learning with Adaptive Regularization

Sampled topic: continual/multimodal learning. Checked the algorithm, Split MNIST/notMNIST/CIFAR/Omniglot results, eight-task continuous-control RL experiment and supplementary sensitivity. UCL groups posterior uncertainty by node and uses stability/plasticity regularizers. On Split MNIST it reports 99.7% mean accuracy over five tasks, matching HAT and modestly exceeding SI/VCL+coreset; on notMNIST it reports 95.7%, and on Split CIFAR-100 63.4% versus 60.5% EWC and 60.0% SI. The CNN experiments average eight random seeds. On its eight-task continuous-control sequence, UCL exceeds EWC/fine-tuning in the plotted accumulated normalized reward.

The evidence is not a parameter-free win. The supplement explicitly shows that initial uncertainty σ and β jointly govern exploration, capacity and forgetting: a lower initial uncertainty reduced rewards on some RL tasks and made the last task difficult even with the forgetting-control setting. The headline supervised comparisons use multi-headed outputs; not every baseline is available in every architecture/data setting (e.g. no VCL result for the CNN vision experiments). This supports a conditional algorithm result, not broad continual-learning superiority independent of task order, head structure, model or hyperparameter selection.

## 1612.02485v1 — Comparative Evaluation of Big-Data Systems on Scientific Image Analytics Workloads

Sampled topic: databases. Checked the two full scientific pipelines, system implementations, end-to-end scaling, individual-operation results and tuning discussion. The study compares SciDB, Myria, Spark, Dask and TensorFlow on one neuroscience diffusion-MRI pipeline and one astronomy image pipeline. The systems have no stable universal ranking: for end-to-end pipelines Dask is at most 14% faster than Spark/Myria in the reported setting, is 60% slower on the single-subject case, then becomes faster as data grows; Myria achieves near-linear scaling; Dask scheduler work-stealing becomes less efficient as node count rises. At one operation TensorFlow is orders of magnitude slower due to array reshape/conversion, while SciDB is fastest on small mean computations. SciDB's unoptimized astronomy iterative co-add is over an order of magnitude slower, while the paper notes prior iterative-engine optimizations could change that result. Tuning and format conversion matter materially.

This is a useful apples-to-apples study of two real scientific workloads, but only two pipelines on 2016-era systems and AWS instances; the authors tuned implementations, and the SciDB co-add result depends on the evaluated release. It does not establish rankings for modern engine versions, other data modalities or untuned novice use.

## 1707.05172v1 — An Empirical Analysis of the Influence of Fault Space on Search-Based Automated Program Repair

Sampled topic: program repair/verification. Checked fault-space manipulation, coverage measures, test augmentation and threats. On GenProg/Defects4J, more accurate suspicious-location rankings correlate with more correct repairs and fewer candidate patches searched. Across eligible bugs, negative-mutation coverage has the strongest reported relation to search efficiency; it measures whether passing tests kill mutants on failing-test execution lines. Test generation improved repair on selected examples: for Math 5, candidate validations fell from 306 to 28 after the fault-space ranking improved; for Chart 1, GenProg found a correct patch after augmentation when the original setup timed out. The counterexamples matter: Math 50/70/73 already rank the faulty line first and do not improve; Math 85's better localization does not change the result because a plausible patch is generated at a clean statement; Math 53's test augmentation makes localization worse.

The study is restricted to GenProg, Java, four Defects4J projects/224 program versions, and uses the developer patch's changed lines as the localization oracle—even though multiple valid fixes may touch different lines. Generated failing tests are hard to obtain. Reported correlation is not itself proof that a new localization method will causally improve a different repair system.

## 1901.10673v1 — Invariant Feature Mappings for Generalizing Affordance Understanding Using Regularized Metric Learning

Sampled topic: robotics/embodied AI. Checked dataset, cross-validation/splits, class metrics and qualitative feature-localization discussion. On 265 RGB-D images of everyday objects and 23 affordance labels, LMCA-R's mean F1 is 0.70 versus 0.63 for kNN and 0.69 for the CNN-SVM baseline; the paper reports 25 random 70/30 splits and five-fold tuning. The contrast between accuracy and F1 is important: for “spraying,” accuracy is about 96% but best F1 only 0.56, owing to class imbalance. Results also vary sharply by category and feature source: the authors report poor/noisy visual explanations for hanging and loop grasping, and attribute some failures to Kinect depth noise and too few useful examples.

The work measures image-level affordance classification and visual feature consistency, not successful manipulation in a novel cluttered environment. Images were captured on flat uncluttered surfaces; affordance labels are acknowledged as vague/non-binary. The high-level claim that learned abstractions aid action substitution therefore remains an untested transfer hypothesis here.

## Candidate cross-paper questions

1. **APR inputs and diagnosis:** A 2017 search-based study finds repair depends on where the fault localizer puts the bug; a 2025 LLM study finds bug reports help repair transformed code but generally do not restore original-set performance; a 2026 study finds representation sensitivity differs between older Defects4J and newer GitBug-Java. These are different systems/outcomes, so they do not prove one shared mechanism. A useful factorial study would test whether localization/context quality changes the transformation penalty, with semantic patch review and project-held-out evaluation.
2. **Continual-learning capacity controls:** UCL reports gains across image and control benchmarks, but its own supplement shows that stability and exploration depend jointly on β and initial uncertainty. Prior coreset evidence in batch 7 also changes by stream balance and model-selection protocol. A preregistered study could compare replay and regularization methods over task order, stream balance and capacity controls, separating average accuracy from forgetting and learning new tasks.
3. **Scientific data-system rankings:** The 2016 benchmark shows engine performance and feasibility depend on operation, data size, format conversion, memory and tuning, not a single global speed ranking. Re-run the same end-to-end workloads on current engine versions and independent pipelines, with both expert-tuned and out-of-box conditions and explicit memory/quality constraints.

These are falsifiable research directions from a small random-arm audit, not global novelty claims.
