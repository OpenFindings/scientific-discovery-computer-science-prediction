# CS breakthrough radar

This is a ranked research-work queue, not a list of proven breakthroughs. The order prioritizes testability and information gained per unit effort. Paper 3 scores are unavailable until leakage-free historical CS outcomes are built; Paper 4 scores remain explicit planning estimates, not validated success probabilities.

| Rank | Lead | What the evidence says | Smallest informative next test | Confidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SQLancer++ feedback / DBMS guidance | In a second fresh preregistered block (4121–4125), cgroup-v2 CPU counters showed higher successful-query-pair throughput with feedback on in all five pairs: +93.15, +43.99, +86.75, +73.08, +57.34 pairs/CPU-second (mean +70.86; two-sided exact sign test p=0.0625). This adds CPU normalization, but arm order was fixed off-then-on and no bugs were triaged. | Run a randomized-order, equal-CPU budget study; if still promising, deduplicate and independently reproduce NoREC discrepancies before claiming testing effectiveness. | Moderate evidence for a directional operational proxy in this pinned setup; low evidence for bug discovery. Small n, fixed run order, and no distinct bug yield. Not evidence Paper 3 predicts the question or Paper 4 plans better. |
| 2 | Finding 158: regime-aware adaptive DP accounting | Existing literature motivates guarding approximate filters outside theorem assumptions. A bounded implementation check is plausible, but this repo has not independently reproduced a mathematical failure or established novelty. | Pin accountant/filter implementations and the exact theorem domains; enumerate mechanism sequences at/near validity boundaries; compare only formally valid conservative accountants and published filters. | Low; a valid fallback may be useful engineering, not a new theorem. Do not claim a mathematical result. |
| 3 | Finding 157: dependence-aware LDP allocation | Private dependence testing and directed LDP leakage are separate established components; symmetric dependence summaries can miss attack-specific leakage in studied setups. Central-DP screening is not an LDP guarantee. | First formalize composed privacy accounting; then test an explicitly synthetic feasibility benchmark and held-out shift, against uniform, MI/Pearson, and oracle allocation. Use directed attacks and report utility/leakage separately. | Low; broad novelty is contradicted; real datasets and calibrated attacks needed. |
| 4 | Finding 155: code-review file order | One controlled study reports defect-class-specific position effects; an observational large field study uses a noncausal post-review-fix proxy. Actual inspection order and defect outcomes are not interchangeable. | In a consenting team, randomize file order, instrument viewed files/time, blind-adjudicate seeded and real defects, stratify defect class. | Moderate for some seeded settings; low for a general field causal effect. Requires collaboration. |
| 5 | Finding 156: supply-chain controls vs outcomes | Audited papers describe developer experiences and reported security failures; these do not show that stronger policy/configuration reduces verified incidents. | Link time-stamped control adoption to independently verified incidents with exposure denominators and confounder controls; use repository/public advisory sources only after measurement audit. | Low; external longitudinal outcome data required; configuration is not attack prevention. |

## Paper 3 and Paper 4 status

- Paper 3: the transfer memo records a 980-question astronomy benchmark and temporal protocol, but no CS question dataset with verified historical snapshots and future labels is currently validated. Do not reuse astronomy effect sizes.
- Paper 4: the documented evidence-graph/action-ranking method can structure tests, but no historical action-level ground truth is available here. Do not call expected utility observed scientific value.
- Candidate selection can change after fresh evidence. If the DBMS proxy replication fails, preserve that negative result and rerank rather than extend the run until it turns positive.
