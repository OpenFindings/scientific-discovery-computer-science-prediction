## Key players: stated plans, deployments, funding and valuations

### Takeaway
Capital is concentrated in a few US "robot brain" and humanoid firms valued at $5–40B with little disclosed revenue: Figure at $39B, Skild above $14B, Physical Intelligence around $11B (reported), and Apptronik around $5B+. Chinese firms instead lead on disclosed units and revenue, and they are now going public: Unitree had about 5,500 humanoids and ¥1.7B revenue in 2025 and IPO'd at roughly $50B+. Among Western humanoid makers, only Agility has a disclosed paid commercial contract record, and its revenue is tiny (about $1.8M in 2025 net sales).

### Inferences
- Valuations have come apart from disclosed revenue. Figure ($39B), Skild ($14B+) and PI (about $11B, reported) are priced on expected platform economics. Unitree at about $50B on ¥1.7B revenue is priced on growth expectations and the scarcity of listed humanoid stocks in China.
- There is a geographic split. US firms lead in model labs and capital, and they disclose capacity and orders. Chinese firms lead in disclosed shipments, which mostly go to research, education and display buyers.
- "Robot brain" companies (PI, Skild, DeepMind, NVIDIA GR00T) are positioning as cross-embodiment platforms licensed to hardware makers: PI with Weave and Ultra, DeepMind with Apptronik and Atlas, NVIDIA with the Unitree reference robot.

## Verified real-world deployments versus demos

### Takeaway
Real humanoid volume in 2025–H1 2026 is about 13,000 (2025, Omdia) and about 19,000–25,000 (H1 2026) units worldwide. More than 80–97% of it is Chinese, and a large share goes to research, education, display and "data collection" rather than productive work. The best-documented paid industrial work in the West is small: Agility's 140,000 totes and 65,000 operating hours across nine sites, and Figure's BMW pilot. No home humanoid delivery had been independently verified as of mid-2026, and the leading home product relies on teleoperation.

## Expert forecasts and sceptical takes; timelines for general-purpose household and factory robots

### Takeaway
Optimists put autonomous household robots around 2030: Sergey Levine's median is 2030, Huang says the "ChatGPT moment" is "nearly here", and one CEO claims 10% of US households by 2030. Brooks, an experienced sceptic with a public dated scorecard, predicts that deployable dexterity remains "pathetic" beyond 2036 and that walking humanoids stay unsafe near people without new mechanical designs. ## Historical analogies: self-driving timelines and hype cycles

### Takeaway
Commentators, especially Brooks, read robotaxis as the template. Timelines stayed "5–10 years away" for 15 years, the definition drifted (personal cars became geofenced taxis with remote assistance), most early players died, and the survivor (Waymo) scaled slowly to about 500k paid rides a week in 2026, still with humans in the loop. The lessons drawn are to watch intervention and teleoperation rates, expect redefinition of "humanoid", and expect consolidation.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Waymo had about 500,000 paid rides a week across 10 US cities by March 2026 (250k in April 2025) and targets 1M a week by end-2026. It went driverless in Las Vegas in July 2026. — [Noqta (snippet)](https://www.noqta.tn/en/news/waymo-500k-weekly-rides-robotaxi-10-cities-2026); [Claims Journal (snippet)](https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/02/12/335668.htm); [Electrek (snippet)](https://electrek.co/2026/07/08/waymo-driverless-las-vegas-four-new-cities/)
- [V] Brooks: "self driving car" originally meant privately owned cars with no remote driver. It now means taxi services, and "all of them have remote operators". During the 20 December 2025 SF power outage, Waymos requested human "confirmation checks" at more than 7,000 dark signals, and the backlog blocked intersections. Waymo also uses the gig app Honk (about $20 per fix) to have people close doors on stuck cars. — [Brooks 2026 scorecard](https://rodneybrooks.com/predictions-scorecard-2026-january-01/)
- [F] Brooks's 10-year self-driving prediction: only Waymo and Zoox matter in the US, and "The key metric will be human intervention rate as that will determine profitability." Tesla "has put on a facade of being operational." — [Brooks 2026 scorecard](https://rodneybrooks.com/predictions-scorecard-2026-january-01/)
- [V] Brooks draws the analogy directly: "what it means to be a humanoid robot will change over time … As with self driving cars, most of the early players … will quietly shut up shop." — [Brooks 2026 scorecard](https://rodneybrooks.com/predictions-scorecard-2026-january-01/)
- [V] Brooks on Tesla: "If driving with three controls … are too hard to make actually work safely for real, how hard can it be to have a program control a heavy unstable balancing platform with around 80 joints." — [Brooks 2026 scorecard](https://rodneybrooks.com/predictions-scorecard-2026-january-01/)
- [V] A commentary on the self-driving timeline: "It's been five to 10 years for 15 years." A counterpoint from an A3 Humanoid Robot Forum panel says AV progress enabled humanoid robotics. — [Automate.org (snippet)](https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-insights/the-arc-of-ventureis-long-but-it-bends-toward-pragmatism)

- [V] Brooks's own Rethink Robotics delivered "thousands" of Baxter/Sawyer upper-body humanoids from 2012 to 2018. One Sawyer was shut down in late 2025 after 35,236 hours. Rethink later failed. — [Brooks 2026 scorecard](https://rodneybrooks.com/predictions-scorecard-2026-january-01/)

### Inferences
- The robotaxi-to-humanoid mapping is roughly: safety-driver and remote-assist ratio maps to teleoperation and "Expert Mode" ratio; geofenced cities map to structured factory cells; Cruise's exit maps to an expected humanoid shake-out (150+ Chinese firms).
- Waymo needed about 15 years from Google's 2009 project to 500k rides a week. If humanoids follow a similar arc from about 2022–23, broad unstructured deployment would come in the late 2030s. This is an analogy, not evidence.

### Gaps
- No quantitative humanoid "intervention rate" disclosures exist publicly. 1X's Expert Mode share is unknown, and an unverified estimate puts autonomous completion at 60–70%.
